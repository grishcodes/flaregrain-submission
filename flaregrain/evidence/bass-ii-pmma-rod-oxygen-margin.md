# BASS-II: an acrylic rod kept burning at lower oxygen in microgravity

**Evidence item 1 · NASA source checked · Flaregrain reading marked as ours**

## What NASA reports

In the Burning and Suppression of Solids – II (BASS-II) experiment on the International Space Station, NASA
researchers burned 24 PMMA (acrylic) rods in concurrent, stagnation-point flow and measured the conditions at which
the flames blew off or quenched.

For a ¼-inch PMMA rod, NASA reports:

| Quantity | Value | Condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Steady flame spreading observed in microgravity (BASS-II) | 13.6% oxygen | ¼-inch PMMA rod, concurrent (stagnation-point) flow |
| Blowoff limit in normal gravity (1 g) | 16% oxygen | the same ¼-inch rod |
| Oxygen margin of safety | −2.4 percentage points of oxygen | the difference between the two values above |

NASA's conclusion on the same slide is that normal-gravity flammability limits for this material should be
"de-rated by at least that much". The slide also notes that the minimum of the flammability boundary lies at low flow
speeds, on the order of diffusive flows.

## Source

P. V. Ferkul, S. Bhattacharjee, C. Fernandez-Pello, F. Miller, S. L. Olson, F. Takahashi and J. S. T'ien,
*Combustion of Solids in Microgravity: Results from the BASS-II Experiment*, presentation at the American Society for
Gravitational and Space Research conference, Pasadena, CA, October 23–26, 2014. NASA Technical Reports Server
record [20160000593](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20160000593), slide 18 of 21, "Spacecraft Materials
Microgravity Research on Flammability" (Sandra Olson, NASA Glenn Research Center).

NTRS lists the record with public distribution and the copyright determination "public use permitted".

## Limits of this evidence

- One material, one geometry and one flow: a ¼-inch PMMA rod in concurrent, stagnation-point flow. It is not a
  general rule for other materials, thicknesses or opposed flow.
- The slide reports steady spreading at 13.6% oxygen. It does not say that 13.6% is the lowest oxygen level at which
  the rod can burn in microgravity.
- The slide does not state the ambient pressure or describe how the 1 g blowoff limit was measured.
- Later BASS-II publications were not checked against these figures.
